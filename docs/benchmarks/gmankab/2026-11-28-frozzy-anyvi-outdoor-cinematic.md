# November 28, 2026 · Frozzy AnyVI outdoor cinematic

**Run by** [@gmankab](https://github.com/gmankab)

I built Frozzy's AnyVI branch with the same seven Intel loader and Vulkan guest-memory compatibility commits used in the Disroot comparison. The Linux native build ran on the RX 6800 with RADV 26.2.6, the 1080p guest option, and a 1280×720 Weston window. It loaded a 344.27 MB driver cache from a different revision and rebuilt the incompatible shader store. REA captured passing tests for guest time during compilation and the pipeline binary store. The branch defaults to its regular Vulkan pipeline cache on RADV. [AnyVI revision](https://git.disroot.org/frozzy/kyty/commit/eed0c578eab39717030a9890cc0d3b0220b6e8c5) · [RADV cache selection](https://git.disroot.org/frozzy/kyty/commit/e197a16dc6404167202ae71be5591f68f77ae4d1)

During first-scene preparation, the title averaged **0.04% graphics-engine activity** and **8.76 CPU core equivalents** from 600 to 720 seconds after launch. In a 24-second REA capture within that period, 18 compute pipeline creations each spent **5.06–7.89 seconds** in the Vulkan driver, overlapping across workers. The first outdoor image was visible by **15 minutes 17 seconds**, followed by multiple rendered frames and a cutscene. At 915–935 seconds the title used **5.99%** of the graphics engine and **0.83%** of the compute engine. The outdoor cutscene overlay briefly showed **3.2 FPS** with no active pipeline builds. Nearby REA captures found substantial buffer-readback time on caller threads and continued shader compilation. Camera and pause inputs did not establish player control in that sequence.

The run reached its **22-minute 30-second** time limit during a later part of the outdoor cutscene. The kernel logged no graphics reset or timeout during this run. I kept its driver and shader caches for a repeat on the same executable.

**Warm-cache repeat** The launch used the first run's cache and save. It loaded **491.69 MB** of Vulkan cache data, **2,236 shader modules**, and **1,954 pipeline records**. The first outdoor screenshot was available by **8 minutes 1 second**, versus **15 minutes 17 seconds** in the first run. The Continue prompt was also advanced about 74 seconds sooner. The illustrated loading screen completed **1,800 main-output flips in 60 seconds** with **33.58 ms** p95 spacing.

The 20-second exterior cutscene sample at 505–525 seconds produced **66 main-output flips** at **3.27/s**, with **363.09 ms** p95 spacing. The title averaged **18.00%** graphics-engine activity, **2.73%** compute-engine activity, and **4.22 CPU core equivalents**. In a nearby 24-second REA capture, **11,373** buffer readbacks took **10.35 seconds** of summed caller-thread time. The corresponding profiling zone on the emulated GPU command thread totaled **3.17 seconds**. These times can overlap across threads. Flip timing measures guest main-output completions, so emulator overlay refreshes are excluded.

The warm run went on to the opening credits. The 35-second credits sample at 690–725 seconds produced **38 main-output flips** at **1.07/s**, with **1,123.85 ms** p95 spacing. The title averaged **44.03%** graphics-engine activity, **1.69%** compute-engine activity, and **3.15 CPU core equivalents**. Summed graphics-engine busy time over that window was **406 ms per completed flip**. A nearby REA capture recorded **6.26 seconds** of summed readback caller time, **4.23 seconds** in command-scheduler waits, and about **0.79 seconds** across the two graphics pipeline creations.

The warm run reached its **25-minute** limit during the opening credits. Some later views showed missing geometry and a large green rectangle. REA counted **74 main-output flips in 50 seconds** at 1280–1330 seconds. Camera and movement tests in that sequence gave no reproducible player control, and the story save matched its prelaunch digest. The kernel logged no graphics ring timeout or reset.

**GPU command timing** I reran the same emulator with the saved cache, the stock RADV 26.2.6 driver, and per-submit Mesa tracing. REA verified actual GPU timestamps in a synthetic tiling fixture and then in the title. The same-source Mesa rebuild also produced timings in the fixture. The title used the stock library despite its build warning. The per-submit trace adds CPU and I/O work, so the uninstrumented warm run is the frame-rate reference. [Mesa timing documentation](https://docs.mesa3d.org/u_trace.html)

At 450–460 seconds, the exterior scene completed **16 main-output flips in 10 seconds**. It averaged **17.94%** graphics-engine use, **2.23%** compute-engine use, and **4.92 CPU core equivalents**. The GPU timestamps attributed **478 ms** to mesh draws, **402 ms** to direct compute dispatches, **397 ms** to indirect dispatches, and **113 ms** to buffer copies. These are sums of command intervals. Nested ranges such as rendering and command buffers cover some of the same work.

At 520–535 seconds, direct and indirect dispatches accumulated **964 ms** and **697 ms** respectively. Direct dispatches with a single workgroup supplied **37,924 of 65,571 calls** and accumulated **295 ms**. In a separate 24-second exterior trace from the same run, **10,902** buffer readbacks accumulated **8.19 seconds** of caller-thread time.

The 710–725 second credits sample included a shot of the car on the street. Indirect dispatch intervals totaled **5.04 seconds**. Across all command buffers, the trace recorded **6.99 seconds** of summed intervals. The median indirect dispatch took **3.68 µs**. Just **301 of 16,551 calls** lasted at least **1 ms**, and they accounted for **92%** of indirect dispatch time. **38 calls** each took at least **33.3 ms**, with the slowest at **86.09 ms**. The stock trace records command type and duration.

The stock-driver timing run continued into daylight and exited after **21 minutes 40 seconds**. REA confirmed that the terminal log reported a guest null write. The kernel reported no GPU reset. The camera and movement probes did not establish player control. In the movement probe, the selected background and character regions were pixel-identical before and after input.

I added the bound compute pipeline's debug name to the indirect-dispatch trace in a local Mesa 26.2.6 diagnostic build. REA captured three synthetic dispatches on the RX 6800. The trace retained two distinct original names even when one pipeline was renamed before submission, and gave an empty name for the unnamed pipeline. I kept this change in the local diagnostic branch and started the title with the same translated shader store and a fresh driver cache.

**Named pipeline timing** In a 15-second sample of the later credits at 840–855 seconds, the title completed **10 main-output flips** and used **50.79%** of the graphics engine and **2.91%** of the compute engine. REA matched the Vulkan pipeline names to local shader metadata for irradiance, visibility-buffer gathering, and ray tracing. Six pipelines accounted for **5.51 of 5.94 seconds** of summed indirect dispatch intervals across **120 of 14,009 calls**. **68 calls** reached the **33.3 ms** frame budget, and the longest interval was **215.3 ms**.

In an earlier 15-second car scene from the same diagnostic run, the longest indirect dispatch interval was **4.33 ms**, including calls to the three pipelines that later accumulated the most time. The heavy work varies by scene. The timestamps delimit GPU commands and can include waits for dependent work. These are credits samples with per-submit tracing enabled.

The diagnostic run reached the daytime cinematic and exited after **23 minutes 30 seconds** with a guest null write. The kernel reported no graphics-ring timeout or reset. The run gave no confirmed player control.

**Vulkan ray-query check** REA inspected checksummed records from the diagnostic run's local shader cache. The six slow pipeline names matched seven cached compute variants with **zero Vulkan ray-query instructions**. The shader-store header confirmed the run used AnyVI's default profile, which leaves ray queries disabled. The RX 6800 advertises both Vulkan ray queries and acceleration structures on RADV 26.2.6. With the test harness configured for RADV's four-byte robust-buffer alignment, its three BVH mirror formats passed. Its synthetic traversal case failed the check for recognized Vulkan ray-query lowering. A separate title run with `--native-rt` passed Continue and recorded an enabled ray-query profile in its fresh shader store. [Vulkan device setup in AnyVI](https://git.disroot.org/frozzy/kyty/src/commit/eed0c578eab39717030a9890cc0d3b0220b6e8c5/src/graphics/presentation/window/vulkanWindow.cpp)

When first-scene compilation reached the same six programs, REA found all seven variants in the enabled store. The instruction counts matched the disabled profile and all seven still had zero Vulkan ray-query instructions. AnyVI logged that it left at least one of these programs' traversals to emulation.

The enabled run reached the nighttime exterior. During first-scene compilation at 510–540 seconds, it used **9.32 CPU core equivalents** and **0.05%** of the graphics engine. A separate exterior sample at 850–866 seconds used **3.81 CPU core equivalents**, **26.85%** of the graphics engine, and **1.44%** of the compute engine. REA counted **13,385 indirect dispatches** in the corresponding GPU trace window. Their intervals totaled **2.53 seconds**, with a maximum of **27.38 ms**. The same six previously identified pipelines accounted for **1.84 seconds**. The direction-input samples landed during cinematic shots and did not establish player control. The run used a fresh shader cache and omitted completed-flip logging, so these timings do not give a matched frame-rate comparison.

In the enabled run's later credits at 1151–1176 seconds, REA recorded **13,311 indirect dispatches** and **5.66 seconds** of summed GPU intervals. The same six pipelines accounted for **5.14 seconds** across **110 calls**, including all **65** intervals of at least 33.3 ms. The longest interval was **166.09 ms**. This confirms that the dispatch concentration persisted with `--native-rt`. Shader compilation was still active during the scene.

REA later compared the cached SPIR-V payloads byte for byte. All **seven variants** of the six pipelines matched the disabled run exactly. The enabled cache snapshot contained **2,540 modules**, including **1,218 compute modules**, and no Vulkan ray-query initializations. The run reached its **30-minute limit** in the daylight sequence. Forward, reverse, and camera probes failed to show movement or camera rotation. The kernel logged no GPU reset or graphics-ring timeout.

**AnyKyty shader comparison** I built the offline ray-tracing probe from AnyKyty `c5e446082ec4` and ran it against the same six program hashes using the recorded pipeline manifest. Both the default oracle setting and the optional results mode produced valid SPIR-V for all six. The probe reported full traversal rewrites for **14 of 41 BVH intersection sites** in one program. The remaining **27 sites** in the other five used host ray-query answers inside the guest traversal loops. This measures shader translation coverage on the recorded wave32 inputs. In-game query cost and rendering correctness remain unmeasured.
